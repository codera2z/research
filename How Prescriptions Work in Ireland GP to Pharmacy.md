# How Prescriptions Work in Ireland: GP to Pharmacy

*Research report, prepared 22 November 2026. Based on HSE, Department of Health, PSI, ICGP, HIQA and Oireachtas sources.*

## 1. Bottom line

Your friend is broadly right, but the details matter. In Ireland today, a GP can send a prescription **electronically to the patient's chosen pharmacy** through a secure HSE-approved email system called **Healthmail**. The patient then goes to that pharmacy and collects the medicine without carrying a paper prescription.

Two refinements to what he told you:

- This is an **electronic transfer of prescriptions (ETP)**, not yet a fully joined-up national e-prescribing system. It was rolled out in 2020 during COVID-19. A true national ePrescription Service (NePS) is still being procured and is expected to reach pharmacies around 2028.
- I found **no official rule that a patient must show government ID** to collect. The pharmacist locates the prescription by the patient's name (and normally confirms identity details like date of birth). Government-linked cards matter mainly for **payment schemes** (medical card, Drugs Payment Scheme), not for unlocking the prescription itself. See section 6.

Paper prescriptions are also still fully valid and still used.

## 2. The main players

| Player | Role |
| --- | --- |
| **GP / prescriber** | Examines the patient, decides on treatment, writes the prescription in practice software. Prescribers include doctors, and certain nurses, midwives and dentists. |
| **Healthmail** | HSE-approved secure clinical email service that carries the prescription from GP to pharmacy. |
| **Community pharmacy** | Receives the prescription, checks it is valid, dispenses, counsels the patient. Regulated by the Pharmaceutical Society of Ireland (PSI). |
| **Patient** | Chooses the pharmacy, collects the medicine, pays any charge. |
| **HSE PCRS** (Primary Care Reimbursement Service) | Pays GPs and pharmacists on behalf of the State for patients on public schemes. |
| **Department of Health** | Makes the underlying regulations. |
| **HSE eHealth / National ePrescribing Team** | Building the future national system. |
| **HIQA** | Sets national standards and information requirements for ePrescribing. |

## 3. Step-by-step: how it works today

1. **Consultation.** The patient sees their GP (in person or remotely).
2. **Prescription written.** The GP creates the prescription in their practice software. The message should state whether it is a single or repeat prescription and include the prescriber's name, address and Irish Medical Council (IMC) number.
3. **Pharmacy chosen.** The patient tells the GP which pharmacy to use. The GP needs that pharmacy's Healthmail address, which can be looked up in Healthmail's directory.
4. **Transmission.** The GP sends the prescription by Healthmail, either in the message body or as an attached image. The ICGP confirms that no signed paper copy needs to follow.
5. **Pharmacy receives.** The pharmacy opens it in their Healthmail inbox and matches it to the patient, usually by name.
6. **Patient collects.** The patient goes to the pharmacy. The pharmacist checks the prescription is legally valid, dispenses, and counsels.
7. **Payment.** The patient pays at the pharmacy (amount depends on the scheme, see section 5). For public-scheme patients the pharmacy then claims reimbursement from the HSE PCRS.

**Known friction.** If a pharmacy cannot find the prescription, a common cause is a name spelling mismatch when they search. Online doctor services such as Zava and Superdrug Online Doctor use the same Healthmail route to send private prescriptions to a pharmacy of the patient's choice.

**Controlled drugs.** Prescriptions for controlled drugs can come via Healthmail, but the PSI says the pharmacy must **print** them. Controlled drug prescriptions from an EEA state or the UK are not valid for dispensing in Ireland.

## 4. Legal rules worth knowing

- **Legal basis.** The Medicinal Products (Prescription and Control of Supply) (Amendment) Regulations 2020 allowed electronic transfer of both public (GMS) and private prescriptions via an HSE-approved system, i.e. Healthmail.
- **Normal validity.** Historically 6 months. Under S.I. 73/2024, prescriptions can now be written for **up to 12 months**, and pharmacists can extend a stable 6-month prescription to a maximum of 12 months if they judge it safe.
- **Controlled drugs.** Schedule 2 and 3 prescriptions are valid for **14 days**, cannot be repeated, and instalments cannot be supplied later than 2 months after the prescription date.
- **Emergency supply.** A prescriber can ask a pharmacist to supply in an emergency, with the prescription to follow within 72 hours.
- **GP experience.** A 2023 qualitative study of GPs found Healthmail ETP easy to use, time-saving and good for GP-pharmacist communication.

## 5. Who pays: schemes that shape the pharmacy visit

| Scheme | What the patient pays |
| --- | --- |
| **Medical card (GMS)** | Medicines free of the cost of the drug, but a **prescription charge of €1.50 per item, capped at €15 per month** per person or family for under-70s (HSE figure). |
| **GP visit card** | Free GP visits, but **not** free medicines. |
| **Drugs Payment Scheme (DPS)** | No one pays more than **€80 per month** for approved medicines. Patient registers and shows the DPS card at the pharmacy. |
| **Long Term Illness scheme** | Medicines for 16 listed conditions are free. |
| **Private** | Full pharmacy price. |

The HSE PCRS pays pharmacists for scheme patients. Pharmacies are legally required to collect the medical-card prescription charge, and the HSE deducts it automatically from what it pays them. Medical card and DPS holders can show a digital card in the HSE Health App.

## 6. About "government identification"

What I could verify:

- Scheme cards (medical card, DPS) are tied to the patient's **PPS number** and are what the pharmacy uses to claim reimbursement.
- Healthmail itself is addressed to the **pharmacy**, not unlocked by a patient ID.

What I could **not** verify: any national rule requiring photo or government ID for ordinary collection. Practice may vary by pharmacy, so this is worth asking your friend directly, or checking with a pharmacist.

A new **Individual Health Identifier (IHI)** is being built to underpin future ePrescribing and the National Shared Care Record. That is the likely future "identification" layer.

## 7. What is coming next

- **National ePrescription Service (NePS).** The HSE has been tendering for a national service (reported at up to about €50 million). The 2026 HSE digital plan says procurement completes by end of 2026, and the Irish Pharmacy Union says contract award is expected later in 2026. The government's pharmacy plan targets pharmacy implementation by **2028**.
- **Intended benefits.** A single source of truth for prescribing and dispensing, and prescribers being notified when a patient collects.
- **Supporting pieces.** National Medicinal Product Catalogue, IHI, HSE Health App, Shared Care Record, and a National Electronic Health Record (procurement began in 2026). Community Care Record rollout starts in HSE Midwest from July 2026.
- **Starting in the community.** GP prescribing and community pharmacy dispensing come first, with hospital discharge prescriptions after.

## Appendix A. A worked example

This example is illustrative. It shows how the steps above fit together for an ordinary patient.

1. Mary has a long-term health condition and needs her regular medicine renewed. She phones her GP (General Practitioner, i.e. family doctor).
2. The GP agrees and asks which pharmacy she uses. Mary names the one near her home.
3. The GP types the prescription into the practice computer and sends it to that pharmacy over Healthmail. No paper is printed or posted.
4. Later that day Mary goes to the pharmacy. The pharmacist finds her prescription by her name and checks it is valid.
5. If Mary holds a medical card she pays the small per-item charge. If she holds a DPS card, her family never pays more than €80 in a month. Without a card she pays the full price.
6. The pharmacy then claims the rest of the cost from the HSE if she is on a State scheme.

If the pharmacy cannot find the message, the first thing to check is that the pharmacy is spelling Mary's name the way the GP did.

## Appendix B. Plain-English glossary

| Term | Full form | What it means in plain words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **GP** | General Practitioner | A family doctor. Usually the first doctor you see, and the one who writes most prescriptions. |
| **Prescription** |  | A written or electronic instruction from a doctor saying which medicine you need, how much, and how often. |
| **Prescriber** |  | Anyone legally allowed to write prescriptions. This includes doctors and certain nurses, midwives and dentists. |
| **Pharmacist / pharmacy** |  | The trained professional, and the shop, that checks prescriptions, hands out medicine and explains how to take it. A community pharmacy is an ordinary local one. |
| **Dispense** |  | To prepare and hand over the medicine that a prescription asks for. |
| **Healthmail** |  | A secure, closed email system run for the health service. Only approved health professionals can use it. |
| **ETP** | Electronic Transfer of Prescriptions | Sending a prescription electronically from the GP to the pharmacy. This is what Healthmail does today. |
| **ePrescribing** | Electronic prescribing | A fuller digital system where the prescription is created, sent and tracked electronically from start to finish. |
| **NePS** | National ePrescription Service | The planned national ePrescribing system that is being bought now. Expected in pharmacies by about 2028. |
| **HSE** | Health Service Executive | The public body that runs Ireland's public health services. |
| **PCRS** | Primary Care Reimbursement Service | The part of the HSE that pays GPs and pharmacists for patients on State schemes. |
| **GMS** | General Medical Services scheme | The scheme behind the medical card and the GP visit card. |
| **Medical card** |  | A card that gives access to free GP visits and medicines, apart from a small per-item charge. Granted by the HSE. |
| **GP visit card** |  | Free GP visits, but medicines are not free. |
| **DPS** | Drugs Payment Scheme | Caps how much a person or family pays for approved medicines in a month (€80). Open to anyone ordinarily resident in Ireland. |
| **LTI** | Long Term Illness scheme | Free medicines for a list of 16 long-term conditions. |
| **Private prescription** |  | A prescription where no State scheme pays, so the patient pays the pharmacy price. |
| **Controlled drug** |  | A medicine with strict legal limits because it can be misused, for example strong painkillers. Prescriptions for the most tightly controlled ones are valid for only 14 days. |
| **Repeat prescription** |  | One prescription that lets the pharmacy dispense the same medicine more than once. |
| **Emergency supply** |  | A short supply of a medicine given without a prescription in hand, when the patient urgently needs it. The prescription must follow within 72 hours if the doctor requested it. |
| **IMC number** | Irish Medical Council number | The doctor's official registration number, shown on a prescription. |
| **PSI** | Pharmaceutical Society of Ireland | The regulator for pharmacists and pharmacies. |
| **HIQA** | Health Information and Quality Authority | The independent body that sets standards for health information and services. |
| **ICGP** | Irish College of General Practitioners | The professional body for GPs. |
| **IPU** | Irish Pharmacy Union | The body that represents community pharmacists. |
| **PPS number** | Personal Public Service number | Ireland's personal reference number for tax and public services. |
| **IHI** | Individual Health Identifier | A new unique health number being created for each person, to support future digital health services. |
| **EHR** | Electronic Health Record | A patient's medical history held digitally in one place. |
| **Shared Care Record** |  | A planned system so different health providers can see the key parts of a patient's record. |
| **HSE Health App** |  | The HSE's app, where a person can see digital versions of cards such as the DPS card. |
| **EEA** | European Economic Area | The EU countries plus Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway. |
| **S.I.** | Statutory Instrument | A type of Irish legal rule made under an Act of the Oireachtas. |
| **Oireachtas** |  | Ireland's parliament. |
